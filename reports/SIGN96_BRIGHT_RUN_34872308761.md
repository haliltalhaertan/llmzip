## Outcome-blind boundary

For each BRIGHT task, before rankings were frozen the examples parquet was projected only as `id, query, excluded_ids`; the `gold_ids` column was not read. The unchanged V52 representation was fit on the complete task corpus only. FLOAT96 and SIGN96 top-100 rankings were then generated for 20 deterministic relevance-independent nuisance trials, written to disk and SHA-256 hashed. Only after that freeze was a second parquet projection allowed to read `id, gold_ids` for evaluation.

FLOAT96 and SIGN96 use exactly the same centered 96-dimensional representation. FLOAT96 ranks by centered cosine; SIGN96 takes the signs of those same 96 centered coordinates and ranks by Hamming distance.

`96 bits = 12 active SIGN code bytes` is not a whole-system storage-cost claim.

## Licensed interpretation

The large positive deltas seen on several NanoBEIR tasks do not carry over as a general advantage on BRIGHT's reasoning-intensive domains. Recall@3 is split 4–4 with a slightly negative macro mean; nDCG@10 favors FLOAT96 on six of eight tasks, again by a small macro amount.

This is a useful boundary condition rather than a nullification of earlier findings. The effect appears strongly regime-dependent: sign binarization can create large gains in some ordinary retrieval settings, while on this reasoning-intensive distribution its average effect is near zero to slightly harmful. The correct research target is therefore a pre-outcome predictor of the regime, not a universal SIGN96 superiority claim.
