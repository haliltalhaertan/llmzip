## Outcome-blind boundary

For each task, the runner first projected only `query-id, corpus-id` from the relevance/candidate parquet to construct the query-specific candidate pool. The `score` column was not projected. The unchanged V52 corpus-only representation was fit, FLOAT96 and SIGN96 complete candidate-pool rankings were generated for 20 deterministic relevance-independent nuisance trials, the ranking tensor was written to disk and SHA-256 hashed, and only then was the `score` column projected for evaluation.

FLOAT96 and SIGN96 use exactly the same centered 96-dimensional representation. FLOAT96 ranks by centered cosine; SIGN96 takes the sign of those same 96 coordinates and ranks by Hamming distance.

`96 bits = 12 active SIGN code bytes` is not a whole-system storage-cost claim.

## Licensed interpretation

BIRCO does not support a universal SIGN96 advantage. On these compact but complex, query-specific retrieval objectives, SIGN96 loses on most tasks and on both macro averages. The one clear exception is WTB, where SIGN96 improves both Recall@3 and nDCG@10 materially.

The task-specific reversal is scientifically useful: the effect is not explained by a simple rule such as “binary is always better at low storage” or “binary always fails on difficult retrieval.” Instead, the benefit appears to depend on the interaction between corpus/query geometry and the retrieval objective. The especially strong WTB result is a candidate for mechanism analysis, not a license to tune on WTB and re-test it as if it were held out.
