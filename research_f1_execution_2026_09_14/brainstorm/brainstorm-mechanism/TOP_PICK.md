# TOP PICK: C1 — Variance-democracy, with C2's amendment and C3's companion

**Belief state:** C1 (variance-democracy: Hamming's one-vote-per-axis beats cosine's
high-variance plutocracy where signal lives in low-variance axes) is the best single
account of the *shared slope* — sign gains with m on all four benchmarks (CLAIM) and on
both PerLTQA sections (VERIFIED this session: profile +21.7 pp, events +6.9 pp from
m=16→96). But this session's tests demote it from "the mechanism" to "half of the
mechanism": it does not explain the 20–33 pp *level* gap between profile and events, and
its literal endpoint (hard equal votes) is beaten by a softer influence function.

## The case FOR C1

1. It is the only candidate that turns fact (A) into a necessity rather than a
surprise: enfranchising low-variance axes must help the democracy arm and ~not move the
plutocratic arm. The universal low-m loss / high-m gain across four benchmarks (CLAIM)
and now two query-sections (VERIFIED) is exactly that signature.
2. It dissolves fact (B)'s apparent paradox: both arms agreeing on *which* axes matter
is required, because axis informativeness is geometry and only vote aggregation differs.
No other candidate makes (B) a prediction rather than an embarrassment.
3. It predicts fact (C)'s asymmetry for free: rotation preserves cosine geometry and
destroys axis-aligned anisotropy — the one thing the democracy needs. A mechanism that
needs a separate excuse for (C) is weaker.
4. It predicted this session's M1 outcome directionally in advance (profile crosses
≤m64, events never crosses — VERIFIED).

## The case AGAINST C1 (attack on my own favourite)

1. **The level problem.** TEST-M1 shows profile and events share the m-*slope* but sit
~20–33 pp apart in *level* at every m. A pure vote-weighting story has no query-dependent
term: same axes, same rule, yet a 33 pp gap. C1 explains the shared slope and is silent
on the reversal itself — the very phenomenon the programme exists to explain. Without a
level-companion (C3 or C6), C1 is refuted by fact (F) as surely as the nine dead rules.
2. **C2's interior optimum.** If "one axis, one vote" were the truth, hard clipping
(t→0) should be optimal. It is not: t=1e-3 beats pure sign by +2.05 ± 0.98 pp (t=+2.10,
VERIFIED, borderline). The optimal influence function is *soft* saturation — C1's hard
threshold is an approximation, and the residual +2 pp demands its own explanation
(perhaps: tiny unsaturated products break ties better than coin flips — but then fact
(D)'s AQS result says query-magnitude tiebreaks hurt, so the helpful residual must be
*doc-side* magnitudes; that decomposition is untested and could easily kill this excuse).
3. **Resemblance to corpses #1 and #7.** Dead #1 (variance heterogeneity) and dead #7
(ALIGN: query × high-variance alignment) both lived in variance-land and died
out-of-sample. C1's defense — "it's the interaction with the aggregation rule, and it's
the *low*-variance tier" — is principled but uncomfortable: a skeptic is entitled to ask
for the preregistered out-of-sample win (C3's synthetic protocol or C4's transform on a
held-out benchmark) before promoting C1 above "best of a bad lot."
4. **Quantitative thinness at full m.** C1 says nothing about *how much* Delta any
benchmark should show — LME +10 vs REALTALK +5 vs LoCoMo +7 vs PerLTQA −6 is
unconstrained. A mechanism that fits any level explains no level.

## Resulting stance

Run C1 (slope) + C3 (level: atomic vs mixture queries) as a two-factor account, with C2
(soft influence function) as the functional form that replaces C1's hard votes. The
single cheapest next step that could kill the largest part of this stance is C3's
synthetic single-vs-mixture protocol: if mixture pseudo-queries still favor sign, the
level-companion dies and C1 is left explaining slopes of an effect whose sign it cannot
predict — which is failure. If C4's rank-Gaussianized float recovers ≥50% of the LME
gap, promote magnitude-distrust to an equal partner and demote pure vote-weighting.
