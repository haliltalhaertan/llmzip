## B. WHY THOSE TWO DISAGREE — solved this round
The PerLTQA decline is SCORER-dependent. On identical subspaces:
    arm         standardized?   96      192     384
    qscale          yes       52.98   54.82   51.87   falls
    hamming         yes       49.01   50.68   47.74   falls
    float_std       yes       56.24   57.78   54.49   falls
    asym            NO        53.71   57.30   58.03   RISES
    float_raw       NO        55.03   58.55   60.43   RISES
Cause: PerLTQA archives are small (median 407 docs, min 293) and 8 of 30 hold FEWER than
384 documents. Surplus dimensions are constant-filled (effective ranks 293-381, covering
26.8% of PerLTQA queries) while full 48 bytes are charged. A constant dimension has
sigma ~ 0, and qscale divides by sigma. Unstandardized arms never divide, so they are immune.
=> "bits beyond 24 B do not help" was measured under a DEFECT, not a law.
=> Honest rule: k < n per archive. A fixed global k is wrong for heterogeneous archives.

## G. RETRACTED CLAIMS (ours, corrected by external review, coordinator-verified)
- "No better choice of 96 directions exists" - WRONG. sum_i rho_i = k fixes the MEAN at
  k/n and caps no individual rho_i. Counterexample verified: 96 chosen coordinate axes give
  rho=1.0000 to those and 0.0000 to the rest, mean still 0.1920.
- "12 bytes isn't enough, 48 is" - WRONG twice over: dimensions are not bytes, and the
  PerLTQA evidence behind it was the rank-overflow artifact.
- "The SVD discards rare terms" - OVERSTATED: rare (df=1) terms survive at 0.94x a
  random-96-dim-subspace null; common (df>=50) at 3.79x. The SVD lifts common terms above
  chance rather than singling rare ones out.
- "CODE wins the common band" - DEGENERATE: BM25 scores exactly zero on 97.8-100% of that
  bucket by construction, and both methods fail on ~93% of it.
